Nolan Wells: the grand jury finds no crime
September 21, 2026

What District Attorney Angel Myers-McIlrath found

  • 23 jurors, five of them Black, heard more than 40 witnesses. No one charged. The vote was unanimous.
  • The state examiner and the family’s own pathologist could not name a cause. The grand jury found drowning consistent with the evidence.
  • Wells chose to stay on the island: “not a scintilla of evidence to contradict that.”

What the family’s lawyers did, according to the DA

  • Offered “a seat at the table” to give the grand jury any evidence they wanted. Per the grand jury report, they chose not to hand over more.
  • Agreed to a joint exam of Wells’ phone, then called it off the morning it was due. The full data the DA was promised never came.
  • On August 13, told the DA’s office they had no evidence of a crime. “That fact they chose not to share with the public.”
  • Let the public believe organs were withheld, the phone was wiped, and the DA hid the autopsy. The DA’s words: “All lies, and they knew it.”

Two of the shots came after Stevens had fallen. Stevens was 32, a Marine medically retired as a lance corporal, married two years, with a honeymoon booked for September.

Nicholas Wells, 26, admitted the shooting to the officers who found him outside the store carrying two guns. He is charged with murder.

The government, the media, and the celebrities told one story all summer: white Americans hunting black Americans. The House Democratic Leader demanded a criminal investigation into what a grand jury found was a drowning. Nearly 60 members of Congress signed a letter invoking lynching over a string of hangings, most already ruled suicides; its leaders called them “modern-day lynchings.” A children’s star told five million followers the case would be different if the races were reversed.

The records say the opposite. In the month Nolan Wells died, 28 white Americans were killed by black suspects. 4 black Americans were killed by white suspects. The data is ugly, and almost no one wants to look at it. We are only putting it in front of you.

For eleven weeks, all they did was incite, while the records say the exact opposite. What is the plan? Wait until someone is hurt over a killing that never happened?

Endless incitement. No one held to account. This has to end.

Presumption of innocence. Every accused person named here is charged, not convicted, unless a disposition is noted. Charges are allegations.

How we counted: method, exclusions, and sources

Method. Window: July 1 through July 31, 2026, the calendar month in which Nolan Wells was found dead; his July 6 discovery is marked on the timeline. Cases are placed by incident date; arrests and charges may postdate the window. Every row is verified against court records or official police releases plus at least two independent outlets. Race is coded from booking photos, official booking records, and published photographs, never assumed from names or third-party lists; A victim or accused person of mixed white and Hispanic ancestry is counted as white, consistent with the FBI categories the base rates above rely on; people of wholly Hispanic ancestry are tracked separately and are not counted in either column. every photo tile on a case card links to the source bearing that person's photograph. Case discovery drew on news sweeps, official releases, victim memorials, and publicly circulated case lists; no case entered this ledger on any list's say-so, and entries that failed verification were discarded. Every photograph and record behind these codings was reviewed by human eyes; nothing here was coded by algorithm or assumption. Circumstance comes from court records. A case labeled a hate crime by anyone other than a prosecutor is not coded as one here. Case lists were last re-checked for new charges, dismissals, and acquittals on September 24, 2026; none of the counted cases had been dismissed or ended in acquittal. An independent full-month sweep of mainstream and official sources ran on August 6. Sweeps on September 22 and 24, cross-checked against the National Conservative tracker, added cases whose suspects were charged after the first sweep; each went through the same verification before being counted.

Exclusions. The counting principle is deliberate violence directed at a person: shootings, stabbings, beatings, drownings. A death from a round fired at people counts even if it struck someone unintended; the law calls that transferred intent. A death caused by a vehicle driven drunk or in flight from police does not count in either direction, whatever the charge, because no violence was aimed at anyone; a vehicle used deliberately as a weapon against a person would. Homicides without a publicly identified suspect are not counted. A killing charged as manslaughter counts; a death caused by a vehicle does not, whatever the charge. Cases where race cannot yet be confirmed are held. Both directions run through the identical pipeline. The underlying dataset also tracks other-pairing victims, including a third victim of the St. Paul shooting, plus held, unsolved, and vehicular cases; those rows are recorded but not displayed in this view.

Citations. Case discovery ran on sweeps of mainstream news reporting, official police and prosecutor releases, court and jail booking records, and victim memorial pages including Gun Memorial (gunmemorial.org) and the Gun Violence Archive, plus an independent full-month sweep of mainstream and official sources.
